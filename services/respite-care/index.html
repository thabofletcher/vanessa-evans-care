Respite care for family caregivers

Most family caregivers I meet have not had a genuinely free afternoon in months. Respite is not a luxury and it is not a failure. It is the maintenance that lets you keep doing this.

The people who call me about respite are usually apologising. They shouldn't be. Caring for a parent or a spouse around the clock is a job that would be staffed in shifts anywhere else, and doing it alone is how people end up ill themselves — which helps nobody, least of all the person being cared for.

Respite can be a standing weekly slot so you can work, shop, see a doctor of your own, or simply sit somewhere nobody needs anything from you. It can be a full day, or a run of nights so you can sleep properly for the first time in weeks. It can be a week so you can go to a wedding without spending the whole thing on your phone.

A standing arrangement works better than emergency cover. If I already know the routine, the medications and where things live, you can hand over and actually leave rather than spending the first hour briefing me.

What this looks like

Regular scheduled relief

A standing weekly or twice-weekly slot you can build a life around, rather than something arranged in a crisis.

Full days and overnights

Long enough to travel, attend to your own health, or sleep an uninterrupted night.

Continuity of routine

Keeping the day as it normally runs, so your return is not spent undoing disruption.

Planned time away

Cover for a holiday, a family event, or your own surgery and recovery.

A real handover

What happened while you were out, said plainly — including anything I think you should keep an eye on.

This is probably for you if…

  • You are the only one doing this and have been for a long time
  • You have your own appointments you keep postponing
  • You need to work and cannot leave the house unattended
  • You are close to burning out and know it
